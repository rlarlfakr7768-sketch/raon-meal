Why a charged balloon sticks

A rubbed balloon can stick to a wall that's electrically neutral.
Contact and separation between different materials can transfer charge to one side.
Bring the charged balloon close, and charges inside the wall shift slightly.
Opposite charge becomes slightly closer to the balloon than matching charge.
The stronger attraction from nearby opposite charge can hold the balloon there.
Charge leakage over time, often helped by humidity, can weaken the effect.
