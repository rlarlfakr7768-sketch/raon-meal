How adding sound reduces noise

Noise-canceling earbuds add carefully timed sound to reduce the noise you hear.
Microphones measure incoming noise, and electronics calculate a signal for the speaker.
At your ear, opposite pressure changes can partly cancel each other.
When overlapping waves reduce their combined amplitude, we call it destructive interference.
Steady low-frequency sounds are relatively predictable, making them easier to reduce.
Sudden sounds and higher frequencies are harder because timing and position matter.
