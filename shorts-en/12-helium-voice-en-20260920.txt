What helium changes in a voice

Helium changes a voice largely by shifting resonances inside the vocal tract.
Vocal cords create vibrations, which travel through the throat and mouth.
These spaces reinforce certain frequencies, helping shape the voice's familiar timbre.
Sound travels faster in helium, raising the resonant frequencies of those spaces.
The changed emphasis makes the voice sound thinner and brighter.
This can happen without raising vocal-cord frequency; inhaling helium risks oxygen deprivation.
