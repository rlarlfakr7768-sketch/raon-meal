Why small droplets become round

Small suspended water droplets tend to become round because of surface tension.
Increasing their surface area requires energy against interactions between water molecules.
For a fixed volume, a sphere has the smallest possible surface area.
Reducing surface energy therefore draws a small droplet toward a spherical shape.
Larger drops can deform more strongly under gravity and forces from the air.
On a solid surface, how well water wets that surface also affects its shape.
