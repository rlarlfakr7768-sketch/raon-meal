Why sound needs a medium

A vibrating bell becomes harder to hear as surrounding air is removed.
Sound in air travels through neighboring regions of compression and expansion.
A vibrating surface pushes nearby molecules, passing the disturbance along.
Removing air reduces the molecules available to carry that disturbance.
In a vacuum, sound has no material medium through which to travel.
Light is electromagnetic, so it crosses a vacuum and brings us starlight.
