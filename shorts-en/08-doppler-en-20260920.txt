Why a passing siren drops in pitch

A steady siren sounds higher as it approaches and lower as it recedes.
A stationary source produces wavefronts with equal spacing in every direction.
A moving source compresses the spacing ahead and stretches it behind.
More wavefronts reach a listener ahead each second, producing a higher pitch.
After the source passes, fewer arrive each second, producing a lower pitch.
This change in observed frequency from relative motion is the Doppler effect.
