How iron becomes magnetized

Iron contains magnetic domains, small regions with similarly aligned magnetization.
Domains pointing in different directions can leave the whole object weakly magnetized.
An external magnetic field favors some domains, which grow or change their orientation.
As their contributions align, the iron develops a stronger overall magnetization.
How much remains after removing the field depends on the material and structure.
Permanent magnets use materials whose magnetization resists being easily reversed.
