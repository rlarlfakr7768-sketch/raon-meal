How trapped air keeps you warm

A blanket keeps you warm by slowing heat loss from your body.
Air trapped between its fibers forms a layer that conducts heat poorly.
Small pockets also restrict circulation, reducing heat transfer by convection.
Compressing a fluffy blanket thins those air pockets and changes its insulation.
Water can replace some trapped air and allow heat to travel more easily.
Fiber type, thickness, trapped air, and moisture all affect how warm it feels.
