Why metal feels colder than wood

Metal can feel colder than wood even at the same room temperature.
Their temperatures may match, but they draw heat from your hand differently.
Metal conducts heat well, spreading energy away from the contact area quickly.
Wood transfers heat slowly, so the patch beneath your hand warms more easily.
Faster cooling of your skin makes the metal feel colder.
Thermal conductivity, density, and heat capacity all help determine this contact sensation.
