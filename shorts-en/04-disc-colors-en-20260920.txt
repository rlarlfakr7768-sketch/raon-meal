Why a disc reflects rainbows

A disc's closely spaced tracks send different colors strongly in different directions.
Light reflected from those neighboring tracks overlaps as it travels outward.
Differences in path length determine where the reflected waves reinforce each other.
Red and blue have different wavelengths, so their strongest directions differ.
Tilt the disc, and different colors enter your eyes as the pattern shifts.
Diffraction gratings use this same principle to separate light for analysis.
