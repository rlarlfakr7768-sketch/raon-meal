Why longer tubes sound lower

A wind instrument's pitch depends on resonances of the air inside its tube.
Sound traveling along the tube overlaps with sound reflected from its ends.
Certain frequencies fit the boundary conditions and form strong standing waves.
For an open tube's fundamental, half a wavelength roughly matches its length.
A longer tube supports a longer fundamental wavelength and a lower pitch.
Opening holes or extending the tube changes its effective acoustic length.
