Why bridges need expansion joints

Bridge expansion joints leave room for structures to change length with temperature.
In most solids, heating slightly increases the average distance between atoms.
Across a long structure, a small fractional expansion becomes a noticeable length change.
A joint accommodates movement as neighboring sections expand or contract.
Strongly restraining that expansion can create large stresses inside the structure.
The required gap depends on material, structural length, and the expected temperature range.
