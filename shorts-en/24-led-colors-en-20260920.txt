What sets an LED’s color

An LED's color depends on the energy structure of its semiconductor material.
Electric current supplies electrons and holes near the semiconductor junction.
When electrons recombine with holes, some energy is released as light.
The emitted photon energy is closely linked to the material's band gap.
Higher-energy photons have shorter wavelengths, while lower-energy photons have longer wavelengths.
Many white lamps combine a blue LED with light emitted by phosphors.
