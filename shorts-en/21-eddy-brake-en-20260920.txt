Why a magnet falls slowly in copper

A magnet can fall surprisingly slowly through a copper tube.
The moving magnet continually changes magnetic flux through the surrounding copper.
That change induces electric currents circulating in loops through the conductor.
Their magnetic fields oppose the flux change caused by the falling magnet.
The resulting force slows the magnet, converting some mechanical energy into heat.
A long slit interrupts current loops and reduces the braking effect.
